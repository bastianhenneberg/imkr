Sidebar, popups, mouse and clipboard

Sidebar

A sidebar on the right, like the rail in TUIOS, lists every window with its project (the focused pane's directory) and what its agents are doing, every agent across all windows with its window, directory and how long it has been in its state, and the git branch of the focused pane with its changed files. Agents waiting for you come first. Enter or a click on a changed file shows its diff in a floating pane, o in the sidebar opens it in $EDITOR. Click a row to go there. Ctrl+B b shows or hides it (as in TUIOS), Ctrl+B e moves the keyboard into it, the settings put it left or right.

Below that is the file tree of the focused pane's project (its repository, or else its directory), as in TUIOS: click a folder or press Enter on it to open it, h closes it again, a file opens in $EDITOR in a floating pane. Changed files are coloured, f in the sidebar jumps to the tree, the mouse wheel scrolls the sidebar. Sidebar files in the settings turns it off.

Notepad

Ctrl+B t opens a notepad in the focused pane, along its right side (or in its middle, or at its bottom: Notepad at in the settings, notes_at): for the thoughts that come while its agent works and that you do not want to throw at it yet. Type a line and Enter keeps it; the agent notices nothing. The pane's frame shows ✎2 while two notes wait.

Key
typing, Enter keep the line as a note
pasting into the line; several lines become a note each
↑ ↓ pick a note
Enter on a picked note hand it to the pane's agent: typed in once it is idle, never into a question; the note stays, struck through
y copy it
e take it back into the line to change it
x remove it
Esc close the pad; the notes stay, and a line still being typed is kept as a note

The notes belong to the pane and are saved with it: they come back after an update, a reboot, moving the window to another session, and when an ended session is brought back. From a shell or another agent:

imkr note --pane 3 "check the login tests before merging"
imkr note ls --pane 3

Popups

A popup is a pane outside every window, shown over the active one: a TUI you want at hand without giving it a pane or a window. imkr popup --name pm -- peppermint-tui starts it the first time and shows it; the same command, or Ctrl+B Space, hides it and shows it again. It keeps running while hidden, so the program keeps its state, and it survives imkr update. Keys, pastes and the mouse go to it while it is shown; a click outside hides it. Ctrl+B z makes it fill everything but the bars, sidebar included, and back; its frame then says popup · full. Ctrl+B ! turns a popup into the pane of a window of its own, named after it, for when it should stay, and back: on a pane without a popup shown, Ctrl+B ! makes the pane a popup. On a full popup Ctrl+B ! makes it small again first, like z, as a full popup already looks like a window. The sidebar lists the popups under Popups: a click shows one, or hides it, x in the sidebar (Ctrl+B e) ends it. It closes when its program ends. Plugins build on this.

Mouse

The terminal's title shows the window and the focused pane, e.g. imkr · 2 api · claude.

  • Click a pane to focus it, click a window tab to switch to it, + after the tabs opens a new window.
  • The buttons in a pane's top border split it side by side (┃) or top and bottom (━), zoom it (⤢) or close it (×, asks first). Floating panes have × only.
  • The sidebar's bottom border opens a new window, a new floating pane or the settings.
  • Right click a pane for its menu (split, zoom, float, rename, move to another window, make it a popup, close), a window tab for the window's (rename, layout, move to another session, new window, worktree). Programs that use the mouse get the right click; Shift+right click opens the menu over them.
  • Drag the border between two panes to resize them.
  • Drag a floating pane by its title bar, resize it by its bottom right corner.
  • Drag over text to select it, it is copied when you let go. A double click copies a word.
  • The wheel scrolls back through the history, typing jumps back to the live screen.
  • Ctrl+click opens a link: a URL written as text, or a hyperlink a program marks (OSC 8, like ls --hyperlink or Claude Code's file links). It opens on the machine you sit at, also when the session runs on another one; only http(s), mailto and file links.
  • Programs that use the mouse themselves, like nvim or htop, get the events. Hold Shift to select text in them anyway.

Copying uses OSC 52, so it works over SSH as well. Your terminal has to allow it (kitty, Alacritty, Ghostty, foot and WezTerm do).

Clipboard

Text you select is copied with OSC 52, so it lands in the clipboard of the machine your terminal runs on, over ssh too. What programs in panes copy the same way, nvim or an imkr on another machine reached by ssh in a pane, is passed on to your terminal (like tmux's set-clipboard); requests to read the clipboard are not.

Images

Programs that show images with the kitty graphics protocol (icat, chafa, yazi, timg, mpv's kitty output, …) work in imkr when your terminal draws them: kitty, Ghostty and WezTerm. imkr answers their question whether images work, passes the image data on and places the images where the program put them. They scroll with the text, come back in the scrollback, and hide while a menu or a floating pane covers them. In other terminals, and inside tmux, nothing changes: programs hear no answer and fall back to text. IMKR_GRAPHICS=1 or 0 overrides the detection, the setting images = false turns it off.
